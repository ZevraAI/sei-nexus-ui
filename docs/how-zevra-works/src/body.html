How Zevra works, step by step

Zevra lets people ask questions about their business in everyday language and get answers straight from the company's own databases. This page shows every step: where each piece of information lives, where an AI model is used, and exactly what it can and cannot see. Nothing is left out.

What is a "vector search"?

An AI model can turn a piece of text into a list of numbers (an embedding) that captures its meaning. Texts with similar meaning get similar numbers. Zevra stores those numbers in its own database and compares them to find the closest matches, for example "which of our 54 business concepts is this question closest to?". The comparison runs inside Zevra's database, not at OpenAI.

What is "AI" doing, and what is it not?

AI models interpret and write: they work out what a question means, draft a database query, judge whether the evidence is enough, and phrase the answer. Zevra's own rules enforce: only read-only queries, only approved tables, row limits, masking, auditing. The AI proposes; the rules decide what is allowed to run.

Setting up a new company (tenant)

Discovering a new object from the company's database

Zevra does not copy the company's data. It learns what the data means: which tables exist, what each one is for, what the columns are, and how the tables connect. The same pipeline runs during first setup and whenever someone adds a new table later ("Discover from DB").

Flow 4 of 5

How an agent works

An ordinary question is answered by a fixed pipeline. An agent is for open-ended work ("which suppliers are putting us at risk?"): it decides for itself which look-ups to run, one at a time, until it can answer. It uses the same safety rules as chat, plus a read-only database session.

Flow 5 of 5

How Zevra learns a company's language

Every company has its own words. "Overdue" at Acme means "expected delivery date has passed and nothing arrived". Zevra picks up such definitions from how people use it, but only through a short list of signals, and a definition must earn its place through repeated use before it becomes part of the company's vocabulary.

What learning never does

  • It never changes data in the company's database.
  • It does not learn from every successful query. Only the signals above count.
  • It does not share one company's learned terms with another: they live in that company's private space.
  • It does not treat a learned term as an instruction. The AI is told it is evidence to weigh, not a rule to apply.

Where a learned term shows up next

  • Vocabulary: once promoted, the term becomes company vocabulary. Zevra's word-matching step spots it in later questions and tells the query-writing AI what it means.
  • Concept knowledge: if the term belongs to a business concept, it is added to that concept's text, so the concept-picking step can see it, and is shown to the query-writing AI as "learned business knowledge".
  • Review: administrators can see learned terms and assign a concept to those that have none.
